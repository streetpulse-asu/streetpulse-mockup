Clinical Field Guide
Phoenix Desert Environment • Non-Diagnostic
Oxygen (SpO2) Under 93%
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY IF:
  • Their oxygen level stays below 93%.
  • They are struggling to breathe, or their breathing is very shallow.
  • Their lips, earlobes, or fingertips look blue, gray, or unusually pale.
  • They stop responding to you or pass out.
🗣️ WHAT TO SAY TO DISPATCH: "I need an ambulance at [Cross Streets or Landmark]. I have a person who is having a very hard time breathing. Their oxygen level is reading [insert number]%. Their skin/lips are looking [blue / gray / pale / normal]. They are currently [conscious but struggling / confused / passing out]. They are currently [sitting up / lying down]."
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Help Them Sit Up: Immediately help the person into an upright, seated position, leaning slightly forward. This naturally opens the lungs. Do not lay them flat.
  2. Loosen Clothing: Unbutton or loosen any tight clothing around their neck, chest, or waist.
  3. Coach Their Breathing: Tell them, "Look at me and breathe with me." Have them take slow, deep breaths in through the nose, and blow out slowly through their mouth like blowing out a candle.
  4. Fresh Air: If they are in a tent, enclosed space, or smoky area, help them move to fresh air if possible.
High Heat Over 104°F
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY IF:
  • A body temperature of 104°F or higher is detected (this means life-threatening heat stroke). Call 911 BEFORE starting the cooling process.
  • The person is confused, slurring their words, vomiting, passing out, or has completely stopped sweating (hot, dry skin).
🗣️ WHAT TO SAY TO DISPATCH: "I need EMS immediately at [Cross Streets or Landmark]. I have a person with suspected heat stroke. Their temperature is reading [insert number] degrees. They are currently [conscious / unconscious / confused / vomiting]. I am cooling them down with [water / ice packs / wet towels].

[ONLY IF APPLICABLE:] They have also suffered severe ground burns on their [insert body part]. The melted clothing/asphalt is still stuck to the skin."
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Get to Shade instantly: Move the person out of direct sunlight. If they collapsed on hot concrete or asphalt, move them immediately or slide thick cardboard underneath them to stop ground burns.
  2. Cool the Core Rapidly: Pour cool water over their body or apply soaking wet towels. Fan them as hard as you can. If you have ice packs, place them on the back of the neck, in the armpits, and in the groin area.
  3. Handle Burns (If Present): If they have burns from the hot ground, pour cool (not cold) water over the burn for 10–20 minutes. DO NOT apply ice directly to a burn. DO NOT try to peel off clothing or debris that is melted to the burn.
  4. Hydration: ONLY give small sips of cool water if they are fully awake, making sense, and can swallow safely. Never force water into the mouth of someone who is confused or unconscious.
Desert Hypothermia Under 95°F
Note Desert temperatures drop fast at night. Wet clothing or high winds can cause hypothermia even if the day was hot.
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY IF:
  • Their temperature is below 95°F.
  • They were shivering but have stopped shivering, yet remain ice cold.
  • They are confused, slurring their speech, or cannot be woken up.
🗣️ WHAT TO SAY TO DISPATCH: "I need an ambulance at [Cross Streets or Landmark]. I have a person with suspected severe hypothermia. Their body temperature is reading [insert temp] degrees. They are [shivering violently / not shivering at all and confused]. I have moved them off the cold ground and am trying to warm them with [blankets / dry clothes]. They are currently [conscious / unresponsive]."
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Get Off the Ground: Move the person out of the wind and rain. You must insulate them from the cold ground—put thick cardboard, a sleeping pad, or folded blankets underneath them.
  2. Remove Wet Clothing: Carefully and gently remove any wet clothing. (Be extremely gentle—rough handling of someone who is freezing cold can cause dangerous heart rhythms).
  3. Warm the Core ONLY: Apply blankets, dry clothes, or body heat to their chest, neck, head, and groin.
    • Do NOT put hot water bottles directly on bare skin.
    • Do NOT rub their arms and legs to warm them up (this pushes freezing blood back into the heart and can kill them).
  4. Fluids: If they are fully awake and can easily swallow, give them warm, sweet, caffeine-free liquids.
Pulse Anomalies Normal: 60-100
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY IF:
  • Resting heart rate stays above 140 or below 50.
  • They are complaining of heavy chest pain or severe shortness of breath.
  • They pass out. (If they pass out and you cannot feel a pulse, begin hands-only CPR immediately).
🗣️ WHAT TO SAY TO DISPATCH: "I need paramedics at [Cross Streets or Landmark]. I have a person with an abnormal heart rate reading [insert rate] BPM. They are complaining of [chest pain / severe dizziness / shortness of breath / no pain]. They are currently [conscious and resting / confused / unconscious]."
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Stop and Sit: Have the person sit or lie down immediately so they don't fall and hit their head if they pass out. Keep them calm.
  2. Loosen Clothing: Loosen heavy coats, collars, or tight belts to help them breathe easier.
  3. Ask About Symptoms: Ask them: "Does your chest hurt? Are you dizzy? Does your arm or jaw ache?" Remember their answers for the 911 dispatcher.
  4. Prepare for the Worst: If you are near a public building, send a bystander to look for an AED (defibrillator) on the wall just in case. Do not let the person eat or drink anything.
Opioid / Fentanyl Overdose Critical
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY IF:
  • You have to use Narcan (Naloxone). You must call 911 even if they wake up, because Narcan wears off and they can stop breathing again.
  • The person is unresponsive, turning blue/gray, or making a "snoring/gurgling" sound (the "death rattle").
🗣️ WHAT TO SAY TO DISPATCH: "I need an ambulance immediately at [Cross Streets or Landmark] for a suspected overdose. The person is [completely unresponsive / barely breathing / not breathing at all]. I have just given [insert number] doses of Narcan. I am currently [doing CPR / doing rescue breathing / monitoring them in the recovery position]."
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Check for Waking: Shout their name loudly. Take your knuckles and rub them hard up and down the center of their chest bone (sternum rub).
  2. Give Narcan: If they do not wake up, give Narcan immediately. Insert the nozzle fully into one nostril and press the plunger firmly to spray the whole dose.
  3. Breathe for Them: If they are not breathing, lay them flat on their back, tilt their chin up to open their throat, and give 1 rescue breath every 5 seconds. (If you know CPR, start CPR).
  4. Wait 2-3 Minutes, Repeat: If they are still not breathing normally after 2 to 3 minutes, give a second dose of Narcan in the other nostril.
  5. Recovery Position: If they start breathing on their own, roll them onto their side and bend their top knee to prop them up. This stops them from choking on their own vomit. Stay with them until EMS arrives.
